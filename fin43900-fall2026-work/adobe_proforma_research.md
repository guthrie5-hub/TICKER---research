## Adobe pro-forma assumption set — draft

These are model inputs, not company guidance unless labelled guidance. They are deliberately separate from the saved Week 3 DCF inputs.

| Value | Label | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue growth: 8%, 7%, 6%, 5%, 4% (FY2026E–FY2030E) | Judgment | Recent reported growth was about 10%–11%. I fade growth rather than extend that rate indefinitely because a mature software company cannot outgrow the economy forever. This needs revision after reviewing management guidance. |
| Gross margin: 89.0% each year | Judgment | FY2023–FY2025 gross margin rose from 87.9% to 89.3%. Holding it near, but slightly below, FY2025 avoids assuming continuing margin expansion without evidence. |
| SG&A ÷ gross profit: 38.5%, 38.0%, 38.0%, 38.0%, 38.0% | Judgment | The historical ratio fell from 39.7% to about 38.0%. I assume modest first-year pressure and then stability, not unlimited operating leverage. |
| PP&E depreciation ÷ opening PP&E: 12.6% | History | FY2025 PP&E-only depreciation was $236m ÷ $1,873m ending PP&E. A future model should apply it to opening PP&E, consistent with the ABG engine. |
| Impairment: none assumed | Judgment | No recurring operating impairment line was identified in the three-year history. This is not proof that future impairment is impossible; the model should add a separately sourced charge if one is disclosed. |
| Capital spending: $200m annually | Judgment | FY2023–FY2025 filing capex was $360m, $183m, and $179m. $200m is close to the two most recent years but must be revisited if AI/data-center investment changes physical-capital needs. |
| Tax rate: 19.0% | Judgment | The three reported effective rates were 20.2%, 19.8%, and 18.4%. I use a middle rate, not the lowest year, because taxes can vary with jurisdiction and discrete items. |
| Inventory / inventory days | None | Adobe has no discrete inventory line; this dealer-specific working-capital mechanism does not belong in Adobe’s model. |
| Floor plan / floor-plan interest | None | Adobe has no disclosed inventory-financing line. Do not insert floor-plan debt merely because it exists in the ABG tutorial. |
| Other working capital | Unresolved | Adobe has receivables, prepaid expenses, trade payables, accrued expenses, and deferred revenue. A defensible driver needs a separate historical working-capital bridge; a copied 0.8% ABG assumption would be unsupported. |
| Minimum cash, revolver, and revolver rate | Unresolved | Need Adobe’s debt/credit-facility disclosures before setting a cash floor or revolver capacity. Do not infer them from ABG. |
| Debt repayment and buybacks | Unresolved | Adobe’s FY2025 cash-flow statement reports $11,281m of stock repurchases, but a forward buyback or debt-repayment schedule requires management authorization and debt-maturity evidence. |
| Cost of equity: 10.33%; terminal growth: 3.0% | Judgment | These are the existing Week 3 DCF assumptions. Terminal growth remains below the cost of equity; both require sensitivity testing rather than being treated as facts. |
| Diluted shares: 427.0m | Fact | FY2025 10-K, EPS note: shares used to compute diluted EPS. |

### Partner challenge and response

**Attack received — Adobe revenue growth:** “You label FY2026E revenue growth of 8% as judgment: why 8% instead of the roughly 10%–11% reported growth in FY2024–FY2025, and what evidence would change it?”

**My answer (two sentences):** I chose 8% because it is below Adobe’s recent 10%–11% revenue growth and is a more cautious forecast while AI changes competition and demand. I would change it if Adobe’s next guidance or quarterly subscription revenue stays clearly above or below 8%.

**Attack sent — Microsoft revenue growth:** “Why did you choose that growth rate for Microsoft, and what result next quarter would make you change it?”

## What remains to research before an Adobe three-statement engine

3. Compare the revenue-growth judgment with the most recent annual guidance available on the chosen valuation date.
