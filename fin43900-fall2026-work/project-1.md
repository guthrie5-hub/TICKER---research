## Decision and intended user

This memo supports the Investment Committee’s decision on whether Adobe merits further fundamental valuation work and eventual portfolio consideration. It is an initial evidence record rather than a price-target recommendation.

## Initial thesis

**Recommendation: WATCH–DEFER.**

Adobe’s filing supports a preliminary view of a large, subscription-led software business with recurring-revenue growth and substantial operating profitability. Management identifies Digital Media ARR as its key Digital Media performance metric; the metric reached $19.20 billion at fiscal year-end, up 11.5% year over year. However, this Edition A does not yet establish a market-implied valuation, a required return, or the durability of growth under AI-driven competition. Further work is warranted before an initiate decision.

### Diluted-share evidence

Adobe reports 427.0 million shares used to compute fiscal-2025 diluted net income per share. Its EPS note defines diluted EPS as weighted-average common shares plus dilutive potential common shares, including applicable stock-based awards. The balance sheet separately reports 413 million common shares outstanding at November 28, 2025; the two measures should not be treated as interchangeable. [Form 10-K, p. 48 and Note 15, p. 79](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000003/adbe-20251128.htm).

### Operating-value driver

Digital Media ARR is selected as the operating-value driver. Adobe defines it as the annual value of Digital Media subscriptions and services plus the annual value of Digital Media Enterprise Term License Agreements. Management states that it used this metric to assess the segment’s health and trajectory in fiscal 2025. Digital Media ARR was $19.20 billion at fiscal year-end, 11.5% above the prior year; Digital Media revenue was $17.65 billion, also up 11%. [Form 10-K, p. 33](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000003/adbe-20251128.htm).

## Consequential assumptions

1. Digital Media ARR growth is a useful leading indicator of durable future revenue and cash-flow growth.
2. Adobe can retain sufficient pricing power and customer relevance as generative-AI capabilities proliferate in creative and productivity software.
3. A later valuation will use a consistent debt and cash convention and will distinguish diluted weighted-average shares from point-in-time shares outstanding.

## Unknowns and decision-relevant risks

1. Whether Adobe’s ARR growth, particularly AI-related monetization, can remain durable at an economically attractive margin.
2. The market price, market capitalization, and implied valuation multiples on the selected valuation date.
3. The sensitivity of value to competitive pressure, incremental cloud and AI infrastructure costs, capital allocation, and future share repurchases.

## Research plan

1. Build a multi-year revenue, ARR, operating-margin, and free-cash-flow history from Adobe’s filings and reconcile segment metrics to consolidated results.
2. Establish a valuation-date market capitalization and develop a consistent enterprise-to-equity bridge, including explicit treatment of debt principal, debt carrying value, cash, and diluted shares.
3. Test the thesis against primary-source evidence on AI product adoption, monetization, customer retention, and competitive alternatives; model downside cases for lower growth and margin compression.
